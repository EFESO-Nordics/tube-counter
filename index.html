How it counts

Every tube end shows a bore ringed by its own wall, so bores stay separate even when the tubes touch. The app looks for round regions that stay stable across many brightness levels, keeps those near the common bore size, and checks that each one has a wall of its own all the way round. Empty positions in the bundle and the shadows between tubes are left out. Bundles lit from behind (bright bores) work too.

How it sizes each tube

For every tube the app finds the bore edge (ID) and the outer edge (OD) and fits a circle to each. The ratio ID to OD does not depend on camera tilt. The photo scale changes across a tilted bundle end, so the app fits that change across the whole bundle and compares each tube with its neighbours. Tubes more than about 3 % off in ID or OD are marked red.

To turn this into millimetres with no input, the Measure size step uses ARCore: the phone tracks its own movement in millimetres while the operator moves it sideways in front of the bundle, and works out the size of one tube end from that. Expect about 2 to 3 % on the absolute size (for example ±0.5 mm on a 25 mm OD). Tube-to-tube differences in the photo are much finer, about 0.5 %. With a nominal OD entered instead, the absolute size follows the OD tolerance.

Taking the photo

  1. Square on. Stand in line with the tubes, so the bores are visible as holes, not as crescents.
  2. Fill the frame with the bundle end, keeping all of it in view. Each bore should be at least about 10 px across in the photo; for very large bundles, count in two halves.
  3. Sharp and evenly lit. Avoid strong reflections on polished ends; a lamp beside the camera works better than a flash straight on.
  4. Check the marks with the zoom, tap to fix any, then save.

Solid bars (no bore), heavily crushed ends and tubes far shorter than the rest (deep in shadow) may need a manual tap.
